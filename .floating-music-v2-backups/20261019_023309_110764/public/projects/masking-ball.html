Overview

Neurodivergent individuals often mask their traits to avoid stigma, which can impose substantial hidden effort and contribute to stress and burnout. This project challenges the narrative that neurodivergent people simply hesitate to disclose their conditions, shifting attention toward the effort required to mask and the need for inclusive environments.

Through Critical Making, Masking Ball, an interactive artefact that uses pressure-sensitive interaction, coloured lights, and sound to make masking effort tangible, was developed. The artefact was evaluated in a workshop with eight participants, who maintained specific pressure levels while performing everyday tasks, followed by interviews and discussions.

The findings highlighted the effort involved in masking, the challenges of determining appropriate masking strategies, and participants' willingness to support neurodivergent individuals. The project demonstrates how critical design artefacts can stimulate reflection and discussion about neurodiversity, masking, and more inclusive environments.

Why this matters

Hidden effort: Masking can help neurodivergent individuals navigate social expectations, but may contribute to significant stress and burnout.

Limited awareness: The often-invisible nature of masking makes its emotional and cognitive demands difficult for others to recognise.

Barriers to inclusion: Limited understanding of masking and neurodiversity can hinder the creation of supportive and inclusive environments.

💡 RQ: How can a critical design artefact foster new understandings about neurodiversity and masking with the aim of inspiring new discussions on inclusive environments?

Design

A Critical Making approach was adopted to explore neurodiversity and masking through the iterative creation of a tangible artefact, encouraging reflection and challenging existing narratives.

Design Concept

Masking Ball makes the invisible effort of masking tangible through physical interaction. Participants squeeze the ball to maintain a red light, representing successful masking, while excessive pressure represents over-masking.

Design Iterations

Initial concept: A translucent rubber stress ball with pressure-sensitive colour feedback.

Material exploration: Replaced rubber with crochet and conductive yarn for easier fabrication.

Pressure sensing: Switched to e-textiles after conductive yarn failed to detect pressure; refined material layering to improve accuracy.

Final refinement: Improved electrical stability by sewing components onto fabric and covering them to emphasise interaction.

Final Prototype

A crocheted ball integrating e-textiles, Arduino LilyPad, coloured lights, and sound feedback. Participants must maintain the correct pressure while performing other tasks, making the additional effort of masking tangible.

Feedback States

🟢 Green - initial state: insufficient pressure for the target state

🟡 Yellow - medium state: approaching the target

🔴 Red - masking state: target pressure; the noise stops

🟣 Purple - overmasking state: excessive pressure

Interaction Loop

  1. Squeeze the ball.
  2. Observe the colour and sound feedback.
  3. Adjust the pressure until the light turns red.
  4. Keep the light red while completing another task.
  5. Notice the effort required to discover, maintain, and coordinate the target performance.

Study

Research Approach

A workshop was conducted to explore how Masking Ball could foster understanding of neurodiversity and masking and inspire discussions on inclusive environments.

Study Setting

Date: May 14, 2024

Event: Critical Making: Neurodiversity & Computer Science

Participants: 8

Workshop Procedure

Introduced neurodiversity and masking.

Participants worked in pairs on everyday tasks, such as talking, drawing, and moving around, while one person also squeezed the ball to maintain a red light.

Conducted follow-up interviews and discussions about masking experiences and inclusive environments.

Data Collection & Analysis

Data: Audio and video recordings, interview transcripts, and Post-it notes.

Analysis: Qualitative thematic analysis to identify patterns in participants' experiences and discussions.

Findings

1. The hidden effort of masking

Masking requires considerable effort, including identifying appropriate strategies, adjusting the degree of masking, and maintaining it while performing other tasks.

2. Neurodiversity and task performance

Neurodiversity can introduce additional challenges in certain tasks while also offering strengths in problem-solving. Participants recognised the importance of appropriate accommodations.

3. Awareness encourages support

Participants expressed willingness to help reduce others' task-related burdens when made aware of neurodivergent experiences, highlighting the importance of mutual understanding and supportive environments.

4. From awareness to action

The artefact stimulated reflection and discussion, but participants mainly proposed general approaches to inclusion rather than specific, actionable solutions.

Discussion & Implications

New Understandings of Neurodiversity and Masking

The effort of masking: Masking involves identifying appropriate strategies, adjusting the degree of masking, and continuously maintaining it.

Combined demands: Masking adds to the challenges neurodivergent individuals may already experience when performing tasks.

The value of support: Awareness of neurodiversity can encourage people to help reduce others' task-related effort.

Towards More Inclusive Environments

Foster understanding: Encourage open discussions about neurodiversity and incorporate awareness and acceptance into everyday interactions.

Promote inclusive leadership: Support environments where diverse needs are respected and individuals feel a sense of belonging.

Recognise overlooked experiences: Pay attention to differences across neurodivergent groups, particularly late-diagnosed individuals and neurodivergent women.

Translate awareness into action: Move beyond general discussions toward concrete accommodations and supportive practices.

Limitations & Future Study

Limitations

Understanding masking strategies: The artefact did not fully reveal how neurodivergent individuals identify, adjust, and maintain effective masking strategies.

Limited actionable outcomes: Although the workshop encouraged reflection, discussions mainly produced general suggestions rather than concrete approaches to inclusion.

Diverse masking experiences: The study did not sufficiently explore how masking differs across neurodivergent groups.

Future Study

Explore masking strategies: Investigate how neurodivergent individuals develop masking behaviours and determine appropriate levels of masking.

Support actionable inclusion: Explore ways to guide participants toward concrete accommodations and inclusive practices.

Examine diverse experiences: Study masking across different neurodivergent groups to better understand their distinct needs and experiences.
